## GAN Training Summary

### 1. Dataset Augmentation Overview
- Original dataset size: 2,111 samples across 20 classes.
- Synthetic dataset sizes evaluated: 100, 200, and 500 images per class.
- Total synthetic samples generated (for 500/class): 10,000.

The augmentation aimed to address class imbalance by generating synthetic images using DCGAN for each class individually.

---

### 2. Class Distribution Impact

| Synthetic Samples | Notable Ratio Shifts |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| 100/class     | Ratios ranged from 0.11 (*person*) to 3.12 (*sheep*) |
| 200/class     | Ratios ranged from 0.21 (*person*) to 6.25 (*sheep*) |
| 500/class     | Ratios ranged from 0.53 (*person*) to 15.62 (*sheep*) |

Significant oversampling occurred for minority classes (e.g., *cow*, *sheep*, *bus*), while already dominant classes (e.g., *person*) saw relatively less impact.

---

### 3. GAN Training Metrics (Per Class)

All classes were trained using DCGAN with early stopping triggered at epoch 32. Key metrics:

- Average Generator Loss: Ranged from 1.0169 (*cat*) to 2.3141 (*person*)
- Best Generator Loss: As low as 0.8438 (*diningtable*)
- Training Times: Varied between 7.35s (*diningtable*) to 64.51s (*person*)
- Discriminator Losses: Consistent across classes, ranging from ~1.1 to 1.6

Classes like *person* had the highest generator loss and longest training time, possibly due to complexity or mode collapse sensitivity.

---

### 4. Classification Performance (mAP Results)

| Synthetic Samples per Class | mAP Score |
|-----------------------------|-----------|
| 0 (Baseline)            | 0.6132 |
| 100                     | 0.4993    |
| 200                     | 0.3697    |
| 500                     | 0.1973    |

Conclusion: Synthetic augmentation consistently degraded performance.

---

### 5. Diagnostic Observations

The following potential causes for reduced performance were identified:

1. Low GAN Image Quality: Issues like mode collapse and artifacts reduced the utility of generated images.
2. Distribution Shift: Synthetic images may not match the distribution of real data, reducing classifier generalization.
3. Inadequate Labeling Strategy: Assigning a single label to synthetic images may contradict the multi-label nature of real samples.
4. Saturation of Original Data: The baseline dataset may already be sufficient for the model, making augmentation unnecessary or harmful.
5. Suboptimal Augmentation Quantity: The chosen augmentation scales may not be ideal. Higher quality but fewer samples may be more effective.

---

### Final Remarks

While class balancing through GANs was achieved, it did not translate into improved classification performance. Future iterations should prioritize improved GAN architectures, realistic image synthesis, and better alignment with multi-label classification tasks.
